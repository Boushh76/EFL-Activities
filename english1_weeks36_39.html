Code-switching — read this first

Code-switching means moving between languages, dialects or styles of speaking depending on who you are talking to, where you are, and what you want to achieve.

  • Between languages: “Det var helt random, men lowkey ganske bra.”
  • Between a dialect and a standard variety: Jamaican Patois with your cousins, standard English in a job interview.
  • Between styles: a text to your friend versus an email to your teacher.

Why people do it: to be understood, to fit in, to match a formal situation, to show who they are and where they come from — or because the other language simply has the better word.

Not the same as a borrowed word. Ski, fjord and hygge came from Scandinavian languages, but they are now ordinary English words. An English speaker who uses them is not switching anything.

A question worth keeping: who is expected to switch — and who never has to?

Some linguists use the term only for switching between two languages. In this class we use the broad meaning.
