Building sentences

Part A: join two sentences into one with the right conjunction. Part B: make one idea depend on the other.

Part A – Compound sentences

Two equal ideas

A compound sentence joins two complete sentences. Both parts are equally important, and each part could stand alone.

andadds another idea butshows a contrast yetshows a contrast that is surprising orgives a choice soshows a result

The order of the two parts is fixed. Choose the conjunction that shows the connection most clearly.

Part B – Complex sentences

One idea depends on the other

A complex sentence has a main clause that can stand alone, and a dependent clause that cannot. The dependent clause starts with a word like because, although, when, until, unless, after.

Compare: WALL-E was scared, so he hid. (compound – the order is fixed)
WALL-E hid because he was scared. → Because he was scared, WALL-E hid. (complex – the dependent clause can move)

Relative clauses give extra information about a noun: who for people, which for things, where for places, whose for belonging. These clauses stay next to the noun and cannot move.

Parts of speech

In each sentence, find one noun, one verb, one adjective and one adverb.

The four word classes

NounA person, place, thing or idea.
EVE, Earth, plant, hope
VerbAn action or a state.
clean, fly, is, seem, have
AdjectiveDescribes a noun.
a rusty robot, the plant is green
AdverbDescribes a verb, an adjective or another adverb.
moved slowly, very old, very carefully

Watch out: not every word ending in -ly is an adverb. Lonely, friendly and lovely describe nouns, so they are adjectives. And some adverbs have no -ly at all: fast, hard, well, never, always, very.
Ask: What is this word describing? A noun → adjective. A verb, adjective or adverb → adverb.
