Remember the blog post

“Personal Space in Norway” – Julien S. Bourrelle

  • The author grew up in Canada and lived in many countries before Norway.
  • At Norwegian bus stops, he often sat down in the middle of the bench.
  • Very often, one person stood up and moved away.
  • He learned that two people on a bench is a “full bench” in Norway.
  • He thinks Norwegians felt uncomfortable with a stranger so close.
  • Then he asks: what if you look different? Would you think people move away because of your skin colour or religion?
  • His point: misunderstandings come from different expectations and unwritten social norms. We all see the world through cultural glasses.

Present simple or present continuous?

Both talk about the present. But they do different jobs.

Present simple

past
future
I / you / we / they wear
he / she / it wears
I don't wear · she doesn't wear

We use it for:

  • habits and routines
  • facts – things that are always true

Norwegians wear jeans to work.

I take the bus every morning.

alwaysusuallyoftensometimesneverevery dayon Mondays

Present continuous

past
future
I am wearing
you / we / they are wearing
he / she / it is wearing

We use it for:

  • things happening now, at this moment
  • things that are temporary (only for a short time)

Look! He is sitting in the middle of the bench.

I am wearing a suit today. I have a job interview.

Watch out! Some verbs are about feelings and thoughts, not actions. We usually don't use -ing with them: need, want, like, love, hate, know, understand, believe, mean.
